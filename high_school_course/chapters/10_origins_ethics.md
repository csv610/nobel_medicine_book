# 10. Human Origins, Ethics, and the Future

## Nobel connection

Svante Pääbo (2022) developed methods for studying ancient human genomes and demonstrated gene flow between extinct hominins and modern humans. Allison and Honjo (2018) showed how releasing immune “brakes” can help the body attack cancer. These discoveries raise questions about evidence, identity, risk, access, and responsible innovation.

## Foundation

Scientific conclusions are bounded by samples and methods. Genetic similarity does not define a person’s worth, culture, or destiny. A promising therapy can also have serious risks and unequal access. Ethics is part of scientific practice, not an optional final paragraph.

## Project: Hold an evidence-and-ethics council

Teams receive a fictional proposal for a new genomic or immune-based treatment. One team presents evidence, one evaluates safety, one represents patients, and one examines access and historical harms. Each team must state what is known, uncertain, and missing before making a recommendation.

**Deliverable:** a decision memo with evidence, uncertainties, safeguards, and a plan for revising the decision when new data appear.

## Discuss

Who should decide whether a powerful biomedical technology is used? How should benefits, risks, and access be distributed?
