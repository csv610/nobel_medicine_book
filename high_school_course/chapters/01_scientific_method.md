# 1. How Medical Knowledge Is Built

## Nobel connection

Robert Koch (1905) established methods for linking particular microbes with particular diseases. Alexander Fleming, Ernst Chain, and Howard Florey (1945) showed how penicillin could become a useful antimicrobial treatment. Their work illustrates that discovery often moves through observation, testing, criticism, and improvement.

## Foundation

A claim is stronger when it has a clear operational definition, a comparison, repeated observations, and a result that other investigators can check. A useful explanation must also make predictions.

## Project: Model a disease investigation

Use colored beads, paper squares, or spreadsheet rows to represent samples from several locations. Invent a harmless “marker” that appears in some samples. Have one student classify samples using a stated rule, then compare the classification with the known labels. Calculate false positives and false negatives. Change the rule and see the trade-off.

**Deliverable:** a confusion matrix, graph, and paragraph explaining why a positive association is not automatically proof of causation.

## Discuss

Why did Koch’s methods need controls? Why does a treatment need evidence of benefit and harm rather than a compelling story?
