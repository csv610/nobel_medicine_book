# Explore: Alfred G. Gilman

**Scientist:** Alfred G. Gilman
**Research focus:** for their discovery of G proteins and the role of these proteins in signal transduction in cells

**Subfield:** Cell Biology and Molecular Systems

> **Why this matters:** A cell is not a featureless drop of fluid: it sorts cargo, builds molecules, repairs damage, and communicates through molecular machines.
>
> As your mentor, ask: What structure or molecule is doing the work, and how would you know that it—not a neighboring process—is responsible?

## Read and connect

Start with the [course foundations](../foundations.md) and [safety and ethics guide](../safety_ethics.md). Then read the [extended background reading](../../chapters/1994_Gilman_Rodbell.tex). Look for the question, method, evidence, uncertainty, and later impact.

## Project

Construct a labeled paper model or digital diagram of the biological structure or pathway. Have a partner test whether the model predicts a new case, then revise it using evidence.

**Report:** state your question, show your method and raw observations, include a graph or diagram, explain uncertainty, and identify one ethical safeguard. Do not use human samples, animals, cultures, medicines, or patient information.

## Reflection

1. What problem was the scientist trying to solve?
2. What evidence made the contribution persuasive?
3. What would you want to test or learn next?
