# Karl Landsteiner: Nobel Medicine, 1930

**Nobel Prize year:** 1930
**Laureate:** Karl Landsteiner
**Nobel-recognized contribution:** for his discovery of human blood groups

**Subfield:** Physiology, Metabolism, and Endocrinology

> **Why this matters:** Your body is running thousands of feedback systems right now, even while you sit still and read.
>
> As your mentor, ask: What variable is being regulated, what is the sensor, and what feedback could create delay or overshoot?

## Read and connect

Start with the [course foundations](../foundations.md) and [safety and ethics guide](../safety_ethics.md). Then read the relevant source chapter in the main book: [1930 prize chapter](../../chapters/1930_Landsteiner.tex). Look for the question, method, evidence, uncertainty, and later impact.

## Project

Model a biological feedback or energy system in a spreadsheet. Compare no feedback, moderate feedback, and delayed feedback; graph the results and connect the model to the Nobel contribution.

**Report:** state your question, show your method and raw observations, include a graph or diagram, explain uncertainty, and identify one ethical safeguard. Do not use human samples, animals, cultures, medicines, or patient information.

## Reflection

1. What problem was the laureate trying to solve?
2. What evidence made the contribution persuasive?
3. What would you want to test or learn next?
