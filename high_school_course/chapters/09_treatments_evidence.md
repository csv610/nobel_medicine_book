# 9. From Observation to Treatment

## Nobel connection

Gerhard Domagk (1939) discovered antibacterial effects of Prontosil. Barry Marshall and Robin Warren (2005) established the role of *H. pylori* in peptic ulcer disease. Tu Youyou (2015) discovered artemisinin as an antimalarial treatment. These stories show the value of observation, unusual hypotheses, and carefully tested therapies.

## Foundation

A treatment claim should specify the condition, population, outcome, comparator, time frame, and harms. Randomized comparisons can reduce bias, but no single study answers every question. Replication and monitoring matter after approval.

## Project: Design a fair treatment trial on paper

Design a fictional trial for a harmless classroom task, such as comparing two study strategies. Define the outcome before collecting data, assign participants by a fair rule if people are involved, keep identities private, and include a no-intervention comparison when appropriate. Students may instead model the trial with random numbers.

**Deliverable:** protocol, mock results, bias analysis, and a plain-language summary that does not overclaim.

## Discuss

Why can a dramatic improvement still be misleading? What information should a patient receive before deciding whether to accept a treatment?
