# Introduction 0: Science Is a Relay

## Why begin here?

Science is not a collection of perfect answers delivered by superhuman geniuses. It is incremental progress in human knowledge. One person notices a pattern, another builds a better tool, a third finds a limitation, and later investigators connect the pieces.

People from the past—including famous scientists, teachers, parents, and Grandpa—were not always correct. Respecting someone does not require accepting every claim they made. The scientific habit is to ask: **What is the evidence, what is the assumption, and what would change our minds?**

## Five habits of an investigator

1. Ask a precise question.
2. Make assumptions visible.
3. Propose a hypothesis that could fail.
4. Check it against observations and competing explanations.
5. Revise openly and build on useful earlier work.

## Try this

Choose an everyday claim such as “more practice always improves performance.” List its hidden assumptions, design a safe test or simulation, and write down what result would make you revise the claim.

## Mentor conversation

What did earlier investigators get right? What did they leave unresolved? How could your generation improve the question or method?
